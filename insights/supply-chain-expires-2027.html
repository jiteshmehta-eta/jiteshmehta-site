Your Supply Chain Expires in 2027

For CSCOs in capital intensive energy industries: why you must run a value chain, and the five controls to put in place before the year begins.

By Jitesh Mehta, Independent Advisor · October 2026

In 2012, an LPG recovery plant in Iraq needed a dehydration skid. A local contractor quoted $50,000. The original equipment manufacturer (OEM) quoted $200,000. Which would you order?

Most people would take the $50,000. I recommended the OEM. Its skid kept all the plant's guarantees and warranties intact, and operations continued without interruption. The hard part was not the engineering. It was convincing management and Iraq's Ministry of Natural Resources (MNR) to pay four times the lowest quote. The lesson I have carried ever since: judge every award on total cost of ownership, never on L1 alone.

I have seen that lesson proved for 35 years, on offshore islands, in refineries and on EPC projects.

In 2027, the best chief supply chain officers will stop asking how much they can cut and start asking what they can build.

Supply Chain (2026) becomes Value Chain (2027). A supply chain is judged on unit price and on-time delivery. A value chain is judged on what it protects and creates: continuity, capability and the total cost of ownership. Five controls will separate leaders from followers in 2027:

The Five Controls for 2027

1. People: train the crew you have before you cut it

BMW plans to cut about 20 percent of its senior management roles by mid-2027, with AI helping decide which jobs go. Others will follow. But cutting first removes the people who know which data is wrong and which supplier really delivers. Build their capability instead:

  • Make AI learning compulsory for every employee, through internal programs or professional institutes.
  • Link performance bonuses to real use of AI in the job, not to certificates.
  • Hire only after it is proven that existing staff using AI cannot do the role.
  • If reductions are unavoidable, let go of the lowest performers, not a fixed percentage from every team.

TCS reported more than 217,000 associates with advanced AI skills by December 2025, and JPMorgan Chase is reported to have about 200,000 employees using its internal AI platform daily. Training at this scale takes money and time, and some roles will still go. But capability compounds, and a cut does not.

2. Data: fix the data before you buy the twin

A digital twin running on a dirty Material Master and Equipment Master gives confident wrong answers. At BG, where I was Supply Base Manager, data was cleansed and mined before the move from Maximo to SAP, with Prumatech building a clean material master. On a refinery project where I was General Manager, Materials (PMC), from 2007 to 2010, a complete material master made the MARIAN materials system straightforward to implement. Both times, clean data came first.

  1. Clean the masters, with cataloguers and CMMS engineers for two years, or a specialist firm on site.
  2. Lock the standards for naming, classification and approval, so the data stays clean.
  3. Start predictive maintenance on critical rotating equipment. Prove the value there.
  4. Then extend it to the rest of your equipment, one class at a time.

3. Price: buy on total cost, and know what a fair price is

I have also seen the opposite. In early 2010, on an offshore construction project I worked on in India, a large pipe spool was lost to the sea during handling. Nobody was hurt. The retrieval went to the lowest bidder at ₹1.5 crore. They failed and were paid 30 percent for the attempt. The third lowest bidder, at ₹2.5 crore, recovered it. In total, the cheapest bid cost ₹2.95 crore against ₹2.5 crore, about 18 percent more, plus 17 lost days.

  • Evaluate bids on total cost of ownership (TCO), including the bidder's ability to deliver, not on L1 alone.
  • Make price breakup a core skill. Train every buyer to split a price into material, labour, rent, administrative costs and profit: all variable plus fixed costs. Then negotiate on facts.
  • Put the maximum number of commodities on two-year framework agreements. A framework can lock you in when prices fall, which is why every one needs an exit clause.

4. Supply: stay in control when the world changes

Hold the right spares, not more spares. Spares tie up capital, so rank each one by what its failure stops: production, safety, or nothing. Hold the first two near the asset. At BG's Panna field offshore Mumbai, where I was Supply Base Manager from 2001 to 2005, main power generation turbine components were held onshore, ready to swap. We hoped never to use them, and that is the point of insurance. On Zakum, four land drilling rigs came from China and some fabricated components were lost in transit. The replacements I held offshore were the ones the rigs needed.

Qualify suppliers who can 3D print to your specification, with long term contracts that cover it. The more you have, the less a closed shipping lane can hurt you.

Keep your ERP under your own control in Oil, Gas and Power, on premises or in a private cloud you control. Public cloud brings real benefits in speed and upgrades, and many industries should use it. But refineries, power plants and offshore platforms serve whole populations. Data is the new gold, and their owners should not mortgage it to anyone. Owning your licences and your data also keeps the exit from any vendor in your hands.

Two cases show why. The UK ordered Huawei equipment out of its 5G networks by 2027 on security grounds. Reuters reported in July 2025 that Elon Musk ordered Starlink coverage cut over parts of Ukraine, including Kherson, in late September 2022. SpaceX called the report "inaccurate". I am not against any technology company. My concern is my clients' assets, and Poland's Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski put the lesson best to Reuters: "In peacetime, you want the best product at the best price. In wartime, you want redundancy. You want security."

5. Weather: budget energy by fuel and region, and plan for nature

Energy prices will not all move the same way. The US Energy Information Administration's October 2026 outlook raised its 2027 Brent crude forecast to $84 a barrel, from $74 previously, while expecting US natural gas prices to fall about 9 percent. One average energy rate in the budget will be wrong both ways. Budget by fuel and location, with a worst case.

On 8 October 2026, NOAA said a strong to very strong El Niño is likely through January to March 2027. Check which sites, suppliers and routes depend on rivers, hydropower or flood prone roads and rail, and agree the backup plan now.

Monday morning

  1. Make AI learning compulsory and link it to bonuses.
  2. Test every open role against "existing staff plus AI".
  3. Start the Material Master and Equipment Master clean up.
  4. Move bid evaluation from lowest price to total cost.
  5. Rank critical spares by what their failure stops.
  6. Put the maximum commodities on framework agreements with exit clauses.
  7. Add a worst case energy budget, by fuel and region.

Will you still matter in 2032?

Write down what your function must deliver by 2032, which tasks AI will take over, and what you and your team will do instead. A CSCO who cannot answer this for their own role cannot lead anyone else through it.

A supply chain delivers what was ordered. A value chain delivers what is needed now and what will be needed next, whatever happens.
